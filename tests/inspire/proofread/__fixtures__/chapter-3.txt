Maria was forty-five now — an engineer who'd lived in Chicago for most of her adult life. The fern on her windowsill had been there a decade, maybe longer.

A Harvard study from the early 2000s showed that most trauma survivors recover without professional help. Maria would have agreed — she'd recovered plenty of times without anyone's help.

She thought about Viktor Frankl sometimes. "Everything can be taken from a man but one thing: the last of the human freedoms — to choose one's attitude in any given set of circumstances." He'd written that in 1959, she thought. Or was it 1952?

The morning she decided to leave the engineering firm, the fern's leaves were yellow at the tips. She noticed this — the kind of thing her old notebook habit would have caught earlier.

Chicago in November is not a gentle city. The wind comes off the lake like something personal.
