Maria was thirty-eight years old the morning she dropped the pothos plant off the edge of her desk in Boston... The ceramic pot shattered on the hardwood floor, scattering dirt across her project management notes.

She'd kept that plant for eight years — since her first week at the firm. It had survived three office moves, two reorganizations, and one near-death experience with a winter heating failure.

In 2018, George Bonanno published a paper in Psychological Bulletin demonstrating that "The most common response to loss is not prolonged grief or dysfunction but resilience" — a finding that would have surprised Maria's younger self.

I'll come back to that pothos in chapter three... because what happened next is the part that changed everything.

The office was quiet that Tuesday morning. Just Maria, the broken pot, and the sound of traffic on Boylston Street below.
