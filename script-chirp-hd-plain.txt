You've heard of Quiet Quitting.

But the next trend goes further. Much further.

Quiet Vacationing.

Picture this.

You log into work, schedule a few emails to send automatically...

...and then you hit the beach.

No approved PTO. No out-of-office. No one knows you're gone.

Just you, your sunglasses, and a green dot on Slack.

Why are so many workers doing this?

And is it actually a problem?

Let's find out.

---

So what exactly is Quiet Vacationing?

A recent survey found that a massive share of Millennial and Gen Z workers have taken time off — without telling their managers.

Now, this is not the same as a hush trip.

A hush trip means you work from a tropical location — but you still do your job.

Quiet Vacationing is different.

You take the time off. Completely. Under the radar.

People use scheduling tools, mobile apps — even physical mouse movers — to create the illusion of productivity.

While they are completely unplugged. Completely away. And completely invisible.

---

Before we call an entire generation lazy, we have to ask: why?

We are living in an era of peak burnout.

Employees report extreme anxiety about simply asking for time off.

They fear they'll look uncommitted.

They fear they'll fall behind.

They fear returning to an insurmountable mountain of work.

In cultures where hustle is rewarded and boundaries don't exist...

Quiet Vacationing has become a coping mechanism.

A quiet rebellion.

Against the guilt of taking days off — days they are legally entitled to.

The problem is not laziness.

The problem is not entitlement.

The problem is a system that punishes rest.

---

And employers are catching on.

The rise of Quiet Vacationing has triggered a surge in monitoring software — "tattleware" — that tracks keystrokes, screen time, even random webcam snapshots.

But here's the thing.

If your employees feel they have to sneak around just to take a mental health break...

...you don't have a productivity problem.

You have a trust problem.

Some companies are responding differently.

They're mandating minimum PTO. They're shutting down the entire company for a week.

They're forcing everyone to disconnect — without the guilt.

It looks radical. But it is actually practical.

Because rested people do better work.
